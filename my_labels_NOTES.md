# my_labels.json — methodology and confidence notes

Self-annotated dev ground truth for `samples/C3896.MP4` (340.34s, 3840x2160,
29.97 fps), built 2026-09-27 for local `evaluate.py` runs. **Not an official
label set** — treat as a best-effort draft and spot-check before trusting a
score derived from it, especially anything close to a class boundary.

## How it was built

1. Full-video visual scan: contact sheets (grids of timestamped thumbnails)
   sampled every 3s across all 340s, reviewed by eye for anything matching
   one of the 14 official classes.
2. Two windows initially looked like candidates (a possible jaywalk ~196s, a
   person lingering near a crosswalk ~300-333s) but a finer-grained zoom
   ruled both out: the first was a misread roadside pedestrian-crossing sign
   icon, not a person; the second is someone standing still at what looks
   like a pedestrian refuge island, not on the carriageway.
3. `stopped_vehicle`/`congestion`/`jaywalking` candidates were additionally
   generated by running the actual tracker (`TrafficDetector` + the
   `src/rules.py` functions) over the full video at a lower `imgsz=640`,
   `target_fps=4.0` than the real pipeline (faster on this CPU-only dev
   machine; fine for triage, not for final accuracy) — then each candidate
   window was checked against real frames before being trusted.

## What's in my_labels.json and how sure I am

- **`stopped_vehicle` x1** (131.26-143.18s): originally two candidates were
  visually verified (a confident one ~124-152s, the loaded dump truck; a
  shakier one ~268-279s). Both got superseded after fixing a real scaling
  bug (see below): px/s thresholds in src/rules.py and src/risk.py were
  tuned against a 960px dev proxy but never scaled for native 4K eval
  video. Re-running with the fix applied collapsed the dump-truck window
  to a tighter, better-supported 131.26-143.18s and made the shaky
  268-279s candidate disappear entirely — consistent with it having been a
  scaling-driven false positive (different vehicles' stops merging into
  one blob) rather than a real event. Trusting the corrected algorithm's
  tighter window over my own coarse eyeballing here.
- **`congestion` x8**: taken from the algorithm's own speed-fraction
  candidates (post scaling-fix) largely as-is. I did *not* independently
  re-derive these by eye — distinguishing "standstill across all lanes"
  from "just dense but flowing" isn't reliable from static thumbnails, so
  the measured-speed signal is probably more trustworthy here than my own
  judgment. Spot-check a few against playback before fully trusting them.
- **`jaywalking` x6**: added after fixing the two calibration bugs described
  below. Two of the six spot-checked against real frames and look right;
  the other four are trusted on the strength of the fix (duration/shape
  changed from clearly-broken to plausible) rather than individually
  verified — see "The jaywalking calibration bugs" below.

## The scaling bug (fixed, worth knowing about)

`STOPPED_SPEED_PXPS`, `CONGESTION_SPEED_PXPS`, and every velocity threshold
in `src/risk.py`'s TTC estimator were raw pixel-per-second constants tuned
against calibration/scene.json's ref_frame_size (1280 wide) — but native
eval video is 3840 wide, where the same real-world speed produces ~3x as
many pixels/sec. Fixed by adding `ScaledCalibration.scale` (actual_width /
ref_width) and multiplying every such constant by it before comparison
(src/calibration.py, src/rules.py, src/risk.py). Re-running full-video
candidate generation before vs. after this fix is what produced the two
different stopped_vehicle candidate sets described above — a concrete,
measured example of the bug's real impact, not just a theoretical concern.

## The jaywalking calibration bugs (fixed, worth knowing about)

The first-pass tracker-generated jaywalking candidates were badly broken
(16 "events" on the full 340s video, several 20-40+ seconds long). Root
cause turned out to be two separate calibration issues in
`calibration/scene.json`, found by overlaying the carriageway/crosswalk
polygons on native-resolution frames at the flagged timestamps and checking
exactly which pixels the flagged detections fell on:

1. **A miscalibrated third crosswalk** (`dino_crosswalk_0`, since replaced
   by `near_side_crosswalk`): too narrow and wrong y-range for its diagonal
   perspective, so legitimately crossing pedestrians spilled outside it and
   got flagged.
2. **`near_carriageway`'s right-edge boundary cut through a pedestrian
   plaza corner** (roughly x:[0.83,1.0] in the old polygon): a group of
   people standing/chatting on a raised plaza next to the road — never on
   the carriageway at all — were inside the polygon and got flagged as
   jaywalking for as long as they stood there (driving the 20-30s "events").
   Confirmed by sampling person detections across the two worst windows
   (105-155s, 230-275s): all 137 false-positive detections clustered
   tightly at x:[0.83,0.965], y:[0.386,0.422] — one location, one bug.
   Fixed by pulling the polygon down to the real curb line (inserted
   `(0.83, 0.43)` / `(1.0, 0.43)` in place of the old
   `(0.8203,0.3264)`→`(1.0,0.3889)` edge).
3. **`dino_crosswalk_1`'s top edge started a few meters too late**: people
   walking straight down that crosswalk's marked stripes got flagged for
   the last ~1-2s of their approach, before their y-coordinate crossed into
   the zone. Confirmed visually (pedestrians walking exactly along the
   stripe extension, still above the old boundary line) and fixed by
   raising the top edge from y=0.6531 to y=0.58.

Re-running full-video `detect_jaywalking` after each fix: 16 (broken) →
15 (crosswalk-0 fix only) → 13 (+ plaza-corner fix) → 6 (+ dino_crosswalk_1
fix) events, with every remaining event's duration under ~2 seconds — a
different shape entirely from the multi-tens-of-seconds false positives
that came before. Two of the six were spot-checked against actual frames
(112.58-113.98s: a crowd near dino_crosswalk_1's edge, plausible; 267-273s
cluster: a pedestrian in white clearly crossing mid-block, away from both
marked crossings — the clearest genuine jaywalking example in the clip).
The other four were not individually frame-verified.

## What's deliberately left out

- **The other 11 classes** (`accident`, `near_miss`, `red_light`,
  `wrong_way`, `illegal_u_turn`, `failure_to_yield`, `illegal_turn`,
  `solid_line_crossing`, `stop_line`, `road_obstacle`, `fire_smoke`): none
  observed anywhere in the full 340s during the 3s-interval scan. Plausible
  for one clip of ordinary daytime intersection traffic — the task doesn't
  require every class to appear in every video — but a 3s sampling interval
  could still miss a brief (1-2s) near-miss or similar between samples, so
  treat absence here as "not found", not "proven absent".

## Verification pass, 2026-09-27 (later same day)

Spot-checked all 8 `congestion` events and all 6 `jaywalking` events against
`samples/C3896.MP4` directly (contact-sheet grids of 6 frames spanning each
window, plus pixel-level crops for the trickiest cases). **All 14 hold up —
no corrections needed.**

- **`congestion` x8**: each window shows a persistent, tightly-packed vehicle
  cluster in the near-side lanes sustained across every sampled frame of the
  window (not a momentary snapshot), including the two longest ones
  (139.21-143.41s and 287.99-327.93s, the latter a genuine ~40s jam). The
  earlier caution about not having independently verified these turned out
  fine in this case.
- **`jaywalking` x6**: this took a false start worth recording. The first
  zoom pass on 112.58-113.98s, 183.35-184.98s, and 257.86-259.26s looked like
  false positives — every visible pedestrian in the crop was walking right on
  the marked crosswalk's zebra stripes, not off it. But the crop (native-px
  x:[0,1900]) was too narrow: re-checking with the real `TrafficDetector` +
  `SceneCalibration` at those exact timestamps showed a *second*, separate
  group of pedestrians just outside that crop (native-px x~1800-2050) genuinely
  crossing on bare, unmarked pavement a few meters past the crosswalk's edge —
  a different person each time (backpack man t~113s, white-shirt man t~184s,
  woman in a dark dress t~258s). `calibration.in_crosswalk()`/`in_carriageway()`
  on the actual detections confirmed the crosswalk-users correctly score
  `in_crosswalk=True` (not flagged) while the real jaywalkers correctly score
  `in_crosswalk=False, in_carriageway=True` (flagged) — the algorithm was
  right; the first-pass visual review just looked at the wrong people. Lesson
  for future spot-checks on this scene: a narrow crop around the "obvious"
  activity can miss a second, less-obvious detection elsewhere in the same
  wide shot — check the full frame width before concluding a flagged window
  is a false positive.
